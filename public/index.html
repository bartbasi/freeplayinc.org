The story so far

It started with a red balloon.

On 9 October 2026 we opened a repository called Updraft. A kid lets go of a balloon in a bedroom, and you tilt your phone to fly it out of the house. That was the whole company.

Four more games started in the next seventeen days. Then a deli needed to change prices on its three label scales, so we wrote ScaleConnect. Then a food tracker, because typing in a whole meal should take one screen, and a solitaire collection on the same day. Then the web sites, including the one you're reading, and a map app for hunters.

Chapter one

Six games, all free.

Each one plays offline, keeps your progress on your phone and asks for no Android permissions.

They are built in Godot 4, and every picture, effect, sound and song is made by the game's own code. Five have public source code, and the sixth is still being built. Here they are in the order they started.

Updraft

Tilt to fly

A kid lets go of a little red balloon in the bedroom, and you take it from there. Tilt your phone to ride warm air out of the house, over the valley and beyond.

  • A red balloon or a folded paper plane
  • Sunbeams, kettle steam, thermals and ridge wind

A calm, clever number puzzle. Drag across matching tiles to join them into one bigger number, then keep going and see how high you can climb.

Tower defense where the roads fork

Fair Deal Solitaire

Solitaire where every deal can be won

A solitaire collection: Klondike, Spider, FreeCell and TriPeaks. Every default deal is proven winnable by the game's own solver, every deal has a number you can replay, and the game can show you how a deal is won.

  • 5,000 proven deals in each of seven modes
  • Hints that cost score points, never money, and free undo
  • Three looks to switch between: Lamplight, Classic table and Modern clean
  • A daily deal that is the same on every phone, with no network

Not out yet. There is no download, and the repository is still private. These screens come from the style study.

Chapter two

In daily use

ScaleConnect

Add, edit and delete products on Tor-rey label scales from a web page, the command line, or Claude through MCP.

It was built for one deli with three scales, where it has run every day since November 2026. Every change is a preview first and then an apply. It reads each scale before it writes, and a change counts as done only after the scale has been read back.

Built with
Python, FastAPI and the MCP Python SDK
Ways in
A web page for staff, a command line tool, an MCP server for Claude
Limits
It only adds and edits product records. It never touches calibration.
License
GNU GPL v3 with additional terms. Copyright 2026 Cheese & Crackers, Inc.

At the deli

ShelfReady

ShelfReady takes a supplier invoice, prices it, updates Square and the scales, and builds the shelf cards.

It was built for one deli, Cheese & Crackers in Champaign, Illinois, with Square and three Tor-rey label scales. It has not been tried anywhere else. It shows a dry run of every change, before and after, and nothing is sent until a person approves it and presses Send.

Goes in
A photo or PDF of a supplier invoice. The Claude API reads it, and code checks the math.
Square
It matches each line to an item in Square, asks a person when it is not sure, updates Square first and reads it back.
Scales
Scale prices go out through ScaleConnect. A person accepts every scale warning.
Shelf cards
Word files, ready to print. It renders each one to PDF to check it.
Also
It keeps a Mailchimp fish email up to date, and a purchase history spreadsheet with one tab per vendor.
Built with
TypeScript on Node.js 24, on Linux
License
GNU GPL v3 with additional terms. Copyright 2026 Cheese & Crackers, Inc.

ShelfReady is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or supported by Square, Mailchimp, Tor-rey or Anthropic.

Daybook

A free, open source tracker for food, macros, micronutrients, body weight, resistance training and cardio.

The whole point is speed of entry: type a whole meal on one screen, log a set in one tap, log your weight in under five seconds. There are no ads, no accounts and no tracking, and your data stays on your phone.

Built with
Flutter, for Android and iOS
Food data
13,598 foods from USDA FoodData Central ship inside the app, so it works offline at first launch

Board Game Collector

An app for board game collectors. Scan a game box or search by title, see current eBay US asking prices, and file the game as Owned, Wanted or Evaluating.

The collection lives only on the phone, with no accounts and no tracking. A small backend does the lookups.

Built with
Flutter for iOS and Android, with a Dart backend

MapApp

A free, open source GPS mapping app for hunters. Its first job is to show where the phone is against the property lines, with no cell signal.

MapApp is a working title, and there is no release yet. It will never have accounts, ads, analytics or data collection. What you save stays on your phone.

Covers now
Android, in 2D. Champaign County and Macon County, Illinois.
Views
A map view, an aerial view and a hybrid view, with a public land layer
Position
The phone's GPS position, the parcel it is in, and the distance and direction to the nearest property line
Property lines
Macon County so far. Champaign County has none yet.
Built with
TypeScript, Svelte, MapLibre and Capacitor
License
GNU GPL v3 with additional terms

Boundaries shown by MapApp come from public records. They are not a legal survey. Public land is not the same as land open to hunting.

The rules

Every game follows the same rules.

No exceptions, and no fine print.

  • 0ads
  • 0trackers
  • 0things to buy
  • 0Android permissions
  • 100percent open source

  1. Free, for good

    No ads and nothing to buy. Everything in the game is earned by playing.

  2. No tracking

    No accounts, no analytics and no tracking of any kind.

  3. Fully offline

    The games never connect to the internet and ask for no Android permissions.

  4. Your phone, your progress

    Saves stay on your phone. Nothing is sent anywhere.

  5. Open source

    Every game is free software under the GNU GPL v3 with additional terms. Five have public source code. The sixth, Fair Deal Solitaire, is still being built.

  6. Made in code

    Every picture, effect, sound and song is made by the game's own code.
